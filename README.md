## What you're actually buying for that money

Light Reflecting™ Advanced Skincare Foundation — NARS uses "Light Reflecting™ Foundation" and "Light Reflecting™ Advanced Skincare Foundation" interchangeably depending on region — is a 30 mL / 1 fl oz liquid foundation with medium, buildable coverage and a natural finish. NARS markets it as a hybrid: the brand's own figure is that the vegan formula is made up of 70% skincare ingredients, with more than 81% of ingredients naturally derived.

The brand's claims, stated plainly so you can weigh them:

- **+93% more even skin tone immediately after application.** NARS attributes this to a clinical study of 31 women. Small sample, brand-funded, immediate-result measurement.
- **Improved appearance of skin clarity over six weeks of daily use, even after makeup is removed.** Same 31-woman study.
- Strengthens the skin barrier and is non-comedogenic, so it shouldn't clog pores.

Two pieces of technology sit behind the marketing language. **Photochromic technology** uses a "photo-friendly" powder that adjusts tone as light intensity changes, which is the anti-flashback claim. **Light Reflecting™ Complex** combines light-active micro-prisms with algae extract and Polynesian seawater to push light around at the surface. Whether you buy the science or not, the practical version of both claims is "it photographs well and doesn't go grey under flash."

The formula is free of mineral oil, silicone, talc, fragrance, alcohol, parabens, SLS and SLES, phthalates, PEG and gluten. Key ingredients listed by NARS: biomimetic oat (redness), Japanese lilyturf (barrier and moisture), cacao peptides and milk thistle (blue light and environmental stress).

On reception: NARS's own product page shows a 4.5 average from roughly 2,380 reviews, with 93% saying they'd recommend it. One caveat worth knowing — a lot of the recent reviews on NARS's site are disclosed as product gifts ("gifted by NARS"), so they're not all independent purchases. The retailer pages show a similar 4.5. It also won an Allure Best of Beauty award in 2022.

## Who it suits, and where it genuinely falls down

NARS positions it for most skin types, sensitive included, and one of the brand's artists has stated it's dermatologist tested, non-comedogenic, with "a satin-like, light reflective finish and medium coverage."

That's the brand talking. The more useful picture comes from the mixed reviews, and the split is fairly consistent:

**Where it lands well.** Dry skin, mature skin and combination skin come up repeatedly. Reviewers with rosacea, acne-prone and sensitive skin report no reaction. Multiple reviews describe 11 to 16-hour wear without separating, and the "my skin but better" effect is the single most common phrase in the positive reviews.

**Where it struggles.** Dry patches can be a problem — the finish is dewy enough to cling if your skin is flaky underneath. Several reviewers argue it behaves more like light-to-medium coverage than medium, especially anyone expecting one pump to cover everything. Oily skin is genuinely divided: some say the moisturising elements seem to prevent oil breakthrough, others find it wears down faster than expected.

**The packaging is the most-complained-about thing, and it's not a small complaint.** The pump sits under the cap, which lifts straight up rather than twisting off. Several reviewers report the pump firing product too forcefully and losing foundation to the floor or wall. It's a fixable annoyance, not a dealbreaker — dispense slowly on the first pump.

If your skin is oily and you're after something explicitly fade-resistant, NARS's own comparison table puts Natural Radiant Longwear Foundation ahead of this one at 16 hours of fade-resistant wear. Light Reflecting is the glow option, not the longest-wear option.

## Shade matching: how 36 shades actually map out

This is the biggest practical obstacle, and it's where most articles just paste a shade list. The codes are simpler than they look.

NARS labels shades with a depth letter and number plus an undertone note: **L** (light), **M** (medium), **MD** (medium-deep), **D** (deep), with numbers stepping between them. So L1 Oslo is very light with cool undertones, L2 Mont Blanc is very light with neutral undertones, L3 Gobi is light with warm undertones, M1 Punjab is medium, warm, golden, MD2 Tahoe is medium-deep neutral, D5 Zambie is deep with warm undertones.

The undertone word is doing more work than the number. Two shades one step apart can look identical in the bottle and completely different on your jawline.

Some concrete answers NARS artists have given on the product page, which are far more useful than a generic "get matched" suggestion:

- **If you wore Yukon, it's been retired.** Yulong replaced it as the closest match in depth and undertone, with Mont Blanc as the alternative.
- **If Mont Blanc pulls too pink against your neck and you're a true neutral**, try Oslo for a cooler-leaning light shade, or Siberia if you want fair with warmer tones.
- **Let the foundation dry down before judging the shade.** It shifts slightly as it sets, so a colour that looks right wet can look wrong a minute later.
- **It does not oxidise on its own** — but like any foundation it can shift with excess skin oil, which is why NARS recommends a mattifying primer underneath for oilier skin.
- **You don't need to buy a pump separately.** It ships with one.

For a first purchase, getting matched in-store costs nothing and saves you a $55 mistake. If you're ordering online instead, order the depth you think you are and the undertone one notch more neutral than you'd guess — the neutral middle shades cover more ground.

## Quick answers to the questions people actually type

**Does it oxidise?** NARS says no, with the caveat that it can shift with excess skin oil. Plenty of reviewers claim they see a change, so buy one shade lighter if you're between two.

**Is it full coverage?** No. NARS says medium and buildable. Several reviewers say it behaves lighter than that. If you want full coverage, this is the wrong bottle.

**Good for mature skin?** This is one of its stronger use cases — reviewers over 35 with dry or mature skin are consistently positive, and a NARS artist specifically cited its 70% skincare content when answering a mature-skin question.

**Good for oily skin?** Mixed. Some oily-skinned reviewers find it comfortable all day, others find it wears faster than their usual formula. If shine control is your main requirement, look at the longwear formula instead.

**Is it worth $55?** On the retailer pages, the recurring complaint is price-to-performance versus cheaper alternatives that blur more. Where it justifies the money is if you want a luminous, skin-like finish that doubles as skincare and you're not chasing full coverage. The award and the review average back that narrow case up; they don't make it universally worth it.

**Do I need the primer and powder too?** No. NARS's routine suggests the Hydrating Primer and the Setting Powder for extended wear, but reviewers wearing it alone report 11 to 16 hours. Add the powder if you're oily or need camera-ready wear.
